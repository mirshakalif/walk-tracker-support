# Walk Tracker Privacy Policy

Effective 21 November 2026

Walk Tracker records your walks and runs on your Apple Watch, shows their history on your watch
and iPhone, shows a walk live on your iPhone, lets you plan and race routes, and keeps notes you
make during a walk. This policy explains what the app uses and where it goes.

## The short version

- No account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking.
- Your workouts are stored on your own Apple Watch and iPhone. We never receive them.
- Nothing is shared with third parties. The exceptions are Apple's own services you choose to
  use: route planning asks Apple Maps for the walking way between the points you tap, and
  saving a walk as a route asks it for the place's name (see Routes); a note you say to Siri is
  handled by Siri (see Notes).

## What the app uses, and why

**Workouts.** Each workout's time, distance, steps, pace, heart rate, calories, splits and route
are stored in the app on your Apple Watch, and copied to Walk Tracker on your paired iPhone so you
can see your history there. The copy travels directly between your devices using Apple's
WatchConnectivity, not through any server of ours.

**Apple Health.** With your permission, Walk Tracker reads your heart rate during workouts, and can
read your date of birth, sex and weight to set heart rate zones and estimate calories, and your
resting heart rate (the average of the last 14 days) to work out heart rate zones from it. It saves a
workout, with its route, to Apple Health only when you switch Apple Health on for that workout.
On your iPhone, with your permission for workouts, Walk Tracker uses Apple Health for two things
only: to show a walk going on your watch live on the iPhone (Apple's workout mirroring, directly
between your devices), and to start a walk on your watch from the iPhone.
Data from Apple Health is never used for advertising, never sold, and never shared.

**Live Activity.** While a walk is going on your watch, your iPhone can show its time, distance
and heart rate on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. It's made on the iPhone itself from
what the watch sends it; nothing goes through any server.

**Widgets.** Walk Tracker's widgets on your iPhone's Home Screen and Lock Screen show your recent
walks' distance, time and steps. The app keeps a short summary of the last few weeks in a space
on your iPhone that only it and its widgets can read; it never leaves the iPhone.

**Location.** On your Apple Watch, GPS is used during a workout to measure distance, record splits
and draw the route. On your iPhone, your location is used only while you plan a route, to start
the map where you are. It isn't used at any other time.

**Routes.** Routes you plan, or save from a walk, are stored in the app on your iPhone. When a
route follows paths, the points you tap are sent to Apple Maps to find the walking way between
them, and when you save a walk as a route, one point of it is sent to Apple Maps to name the
place, as in any app that uses Apple's maps; Apple's privacy policy covers that. Your routes,
with your best, average and last times on each, are copied to your Apple Watch so it can
recognise them and race them, directly between your devices.

**Notes.** If you record a note during a workout, Walk Tracker uses your Apple Watch's microphone
only while that note is recording, or, if you choose Headphones in Settings › Notes, your
iPhone's microphone input from the headphones connected to it, only while that note is
recording. The recording is kept with the workout on your watch or iPhone, and copied between
Walk Tracker on your watch and iPhone with the workout, directly between your devices. Your iPhone
writes it out as text with Apple's speech recognition on the iPhone itself: the recording isn't
sent to Apple, to us or to anyone else for that. A language your iPhone can't recognise on its own
stays a recording. A note you say to Siri is handled by Siri, as any Siri request is, under
Apple's privacy policy, and is kept with the workout as text. Notes and recordings are deleted
with their workout.

**Music.** Walk Tracker's own Music page on your watch shows and controls what the Music app on
your iPhone is playing: the iPhone sends the song's title, artist and cover to your watch,
directly between your devices. If you tap the star on the Music page during a workout, Walk
Tracker on your iPhone asks Apple Music to add the song playing there to your Favourites; the
song's Apple Music ID goes to Apple Music for that request only, under Apple's privacy policy.
If you choose a playlist to start with your walk, Walk Tracker lists your iPhone's playlists for
you to pick one, and remembers only the one you picked, in Settings. These need your permission
for Media & Apple Music, asked the first time. Walk Tracker doesn't read your listening history,
and sends nothing to us or anyone else. Music from other apps isn't read at all.

**Watch face.** Walk Tracker's complications show this week's distance and your last walk. The
watch app keeps that short summary where its complications can read it, on your watch.

**Motion.** The motion sensors count your steps and cadence during a workout.

**Heart-rate sensors.** If you choose a Bluetooth heart-rate sensor in Settings on your Apple
Watch, such as a Whoop band with Heart Rate Broadcast on or a chest strap, the watch connects to
it during workouts and uses its heart rate in place of the watch's own. Its readings are stored
with your workouts like any other heart rate, on your own devices; they aren't saved to Apple
Health and aren't sent anywhere else. The sensor's name is remembered on your watch so it can
reconnect. Bluetooth is used for nothing else. You can also choose to have no heart rate in Walk
Tracker at all, in Settings.

**Settings.** Your goals and preferences are stored in the app on your devices.

## Your choices

You can turn off Health, Location, Motion, Bluetooth, Microphone, Speech Recognition, Media &
Apple Music or Live Activities at any time in the Settings app on your iPhone or Apple Watch. Deleting Walk Tracker from a device deletes its workout history from that device.
Workouts you saved to Apple Health stay there until you delete them in the Health app.

## Children

Walk Tracker doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children.
